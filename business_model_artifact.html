The takeaway
Analyzing a response costs ~$0.004. The floor to keep the lights on is ~$100–200/mo.

So usage is cheap and the product isn't cost-constrained — a base subscription should cover the fixed floor, and generous usage allowances handle the rest. That's the hybrid tiered + usage model, and the cost math makes the case for it.

The model

B2B SaaS — hybrid tiered subscription + usage

Charge a base subscription that covers the fixed floor and gives predictable recurring revenue, bake a generous response allowance into each tier, meter overage beyond it, and gate the premium capabilities — emotion, Strengths & Gripes, competitive benchmarking, the closed loop — to build a clear upgrade path.

01

A fixed floor exists

~$100–200/mo before your first heavy user, so a base fee — not pure pay-as-you-go — carries it.

02

Marginal cost is tiny

~$0.004 per response means allowances can be generous and overage stays cheap to grant.

03

Features map to tiers

Emotion, competitive, and closed-loop become the reasons to move up a plan.

Overage: bundle generously, then ~$30–40 per additional 1,000 analyzed responses — roughly 8–10× the ~$4 cost, a strong margin and a clean nudge to the next tier. Margin: Growth at $399 runs ~$40–60 all-in → ~85–90% gross margin. Annual billing at ~2 months free.

Where this lives

Not the product definition — a companion to it

Price points and positioning aren't testable software behaviors, so they stay out of the requirements spec. They live in this document. The two connect by one rule:

The linkage rule. Pricing strategy lives here. When a pricing decision needs the product to do something — meter responses, enforce a plan limit, gate a feature by tier, integrate billing — that capability becomes a requirement in the spec and is traced like any other. Strategy stays out; the mechanics it demands come in.

The spec already records the model type as decision D-7. Metering, tier limits, feature gating, and billing become requirements when we scope them — billing most likely after the MVP, since early accounts can be invoiced by hand.

To validate

Open questions

  • Willingness-to-pay (highest priority) — test these tiers against 3–5 real prospects before locking them.
  • First design partners (D-8) — who are they? Their feedback calibrates both product and price.
  • Allowance sizing — are 1,000 / 5,000 / 15,000 responses the right bundle breaks for each segment?
  • Add-ons vs tiers — should extra competitors, brands, and seats be à-la-carte instead of tier-bound?
  • Billing timing — manual invoicing for design partners now; automated billing is a later product requirement.
